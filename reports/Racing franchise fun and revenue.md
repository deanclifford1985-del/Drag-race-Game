# Owned cars and fair economies outlast spectacle

Need for Speed, Forza and Gran Turismo stay fun for years for the same reason: the player owns a car, makes it visibly and mechanically their own, and climbs a ladder of goals that always has both a short rung and a long one. They make money mainly by selling a premium-priced game to a very large audience and then layering smaller income on top: premium editions, late discounts, new platforms, optional currency or DLC, and partner deals. The franchise that is healthiest in 2026, Gran Turismo 7, is the one that kept shipping small free updates for more than four years and loosened its economy after a launch backlash; the one that is dormant, Need for Speed, repeatedly put random upgrades, online requirements or split progression between the player and the car. Forza Horizon shows that approachability and a weekly drip of small goals reach a far bigger audience than simulation depth does, and Forza Motorsport (2023) shows that locking tuning behind per-car grinding kills a game even when critics score it in the mid-80s. Almost every backlash in these notes was an economy or access problem, not a driving problem. For a small independent drag racing game, the transferable parts are cheap: plannable upgrades that change the car's look and time slip, a named ladder of rivals or classes, a weekly set of challenges, an offline single-player mode, fictional cars and a premium price with optional packs. Most sales and revenue figures below are third-party estimates, because Microsoft, Sony and EA publish very little, and none of the evidence measures how these mechanics perform at indie scale.

## All three franchises run on one loop: own it, build it, climb

The Need for Speed entries that players still measure everything against combine deep customization of a car the player owns, a clear ladder and police pursuit with real stakes. A retrospective sums up the era as "the Underground games laid down the blueprint, Most Wanted perfected it" ([RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/the-underground-era-a-retrospective-on-black-boxs-early-need-for-speed-games-pt-1)), and Most Wanted (2005) is the series best-seller at **16 million copies** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_for_Speed)). Heat (2019) is the best modern statement of the stakes idea: day races pay cash, night races pay reputation multiplied by heat level, and the night's reputation is lost if the player is busted before driving back to a garage ([GameSpot](https://Gamespot.com/reviews/need-for-speed-heat-review-getting-warmer/1900-6417366/)). Reviewers also called that loop unfair in early, slow cars ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2019/11/13/need-for-speed-heat-review-ps4-pro-xbox-one-pc/)), so losable stakes need tuning around the starter car. Customization is the pillar that survives weak entries: critics praised it in the 2015 reboot even while panning the online requirement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_for_Speed_(2015_video_game))).

Gran Turismo 7 builds the same loop as a "car life": earn credits, buy, tune, collect. The GT Café guided players through 39 collection "Menu Books" at launch ([RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/gran-turismo/gran-turismo-7s-new-gt-cafe-is-a-celebration-of-car-culture/)), and by October 2026 the Extra Menus reached No. 57, gated at Collector Level 70, 80 and 90, with engine swaps unlocking at Collector Level 50 ([Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2026/10/15/gran-turismo-7-update-1-72-spec-iv-part-1-patch-notes/)). That gating turns the existing car list into endgame content at almost no production cost. Kazunori Yamauchi frames the series as a way to keep car culture alive, with collecting as "another crucial side to car culture" ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/gran-turismo-7-wants-you-to-love-car-culture-again/1100-6500238/)), which gives the museum, photo mode and designer interviews a purpose beyond racing.

Forza Horizon removes friction instead of adding structure, and its audience shows why that scales. Among Horizon 5's PS5 players, an estimated **89% also played GTA 5 and only 24% played Gran Turismo 7** ([Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/forza-horizon-5-races-past-5m-copies)): the mass market for car games is casual. But Horizon 4 and 5 gave hypercars away in the first hour, which a Horizon 6 review calls "one of the series' longest-standing issues" ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/gamebase/reviews/forza-horizon-6-review)). Horizon 6 (May 2026) answered with rarer wheelspins and a seven-step wristband ladder that raises car-class limits ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/forza-horizon-6-progression-explained-with-wristbands-stamps-and-horizon-play/)). When the most generous studio in the genre chooses scarcity and a visible ladder, the lesson is that easy rewards help onboarding but drain the value of earning a car.

Practitioner analysis of mobile drag racers reaches the same conclusion from the other side. Michail Katkoff argued in 2016 that CSR Racing and Racing Rivals "are not really racing games"; the retention lives in the earn, upgrade and collect meta-game, which works when goals are legible and the player chooses which car to build ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog//2016/01/need-for-speed-no-limits-and-why-racing.html)). The academic support is self-determination theory: competence, autonomy and relatedness each independently predicted enjoyment and intent to keep playing, in correlational studies ([Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)).

## Small scheduled drops and one yearly tentpole keep players returning

The live cadence that works is modest. Playground described Horizon's weekly Festival Playlist as "a snackable and manageable amount of stuff that's new", with more "in a few days", and tied it to low return friction: "the friction to get you back in the game is very low" ([Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/news/xbox/2021/10/11/forza-horizon-5-was-built-entirely-with-xbox-game-pass-in-mind/)). That model kept Horizon 4 supported for about five years ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/gaming/xbox/xboxs-forza-horizon-4-is-set-to-disappear-from-digital-storefronts-after-five-years-of-support-but-you-still-have-time-to-buy)).

GT7 is the strongest evidence. Its 2026 updates added three to five cars every six to eight weeks ([gran-turismo.com](https://www.gran-turismo.com/gb/news/00_8895693.html)), and a five-event Weekly Challenge set with a fixed completion reward has run since November 2023 ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/gran-turismo-7-weekly-challenges-20231108)). Yamauchi said in December 2025 that active users were **"over 2 million people, and the new users are increasing"** nearly four years after launch, adding "We've never experienced this phenomenon before, and neither has PlayStation" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2025/12/sonys-never-experienced-this-phenomenon-before-ps5-smash-hit-gran-turismo-7-getting-stronger-over-time)). He also said active users doubled after the large Spec II update in 2023 ([GTPlanet](https://gtplanet.net/gt7-really-high-active-users-kazunori-yamauchi-20231213)). The reading: the regular drip holds the audience, and the named annual update brings lapsed players back. No studio has published per-feature retention data, so this rests on developer statements.

Cars alone run out as motivation. EA's own review of Need for Speed Unbound's two years of live service found that players responded to new ways to play (drag, drift, playable cops, an extraction mode) and to ranks and leagues, that "single-player content beyond launch matters a great deal", and that one mode felt repetitive once players owned the cars they wanted ([EA](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/games/need-for-speed/need-for-speed-unbound/news/end-of-kaizen-review)). Four volumes a year did not rescue a weak launch; EA ended support in February 2025 ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/need-for-speed-is-on-hold-but-ea-says-it-will-return-in-new-and-interesting-ways)).

Time-limited rewards cut both ways. Horizon's playlist-exclusive cars drew years of fear-of-missing-out complaints, and Horizon 6 now promises missed cars will return later ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/forza/everything-we-know-about-the-forza-horizon-6-festival-playlist-so-far)). Old content is also cheap to recycle: after ending development on Forza Motorsport, Turn 10 re-runs earlier tours and reward cars monthly ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/12/17/turn-off-the-engine-and-retire-the-car-turn-10-signals-end-of-forza-motorsport-support/)).

## The money comes from premium sales first, layered income second

None of the three is primarily a microtransaction business. Each sells a full-price game to millions and adds layers.

| Franchise | Scale | Main revenue layers |
|---|---|---|
| Gran Turismo | Over 100 million units sold as of June 2025 (official). GT7: about 12.7 million copies and $840 million (estimate, November 2026) | Premium sale; credit packs ($2.49 to $19.99); one paid DLC ($29.99, December 2025); car, tyre and wheel-maker partnerships |
| Forza Horizon | Horizon 5: over 53 million players, about 23 million via Game Pass (estimate). Horizon 6: 6 million players in its first week (official) | Game Pass reach; $69.99 standard and $120 premium with paid early access; late discounts; PS5 release after costs are recovered |
| Need for Speed | About 150 million units (secondary figure, unchanged since 2013); over $2.7 billion by 2009 (official, dated) | Premium sale; season passes; one free-to-play mobile game; brand licensing |

Sources: [gran-turismo.com](https://www.gran-turismo.com/gb/news/00_1836979.html), [Instant Gaming](https://news.instant-gaming.com/en/articles/22851-gran-turismo-7-has-reportedly-sold-12-7-million-copies), [Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/news/heres-how-much-gran-turismo-7-microtransactions-cost/), [Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/forza-horizon-5-races-past-5m-copies), [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/forza-horizon-6-surpasses-six-million-players-in-first-week/), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/forza-horizon-6-game-pass-early-access-price-increase-premium-edition-60-120-2000662606), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_for_Speed), [EA press release, 2009](https://s204.q4cdn.com/701424631/files/doc_news/2009/10/1/417082.pdf).

Forza's pattern is sequential, and each step reaches a more price-sensitive buyer. Horizon 6 drew about **1.4 million players into paid early access** at $60 to $120 each ([Kitguru](https://www.kitguru.net/tech-news/mustafa-mahmoud/forza-horizon-6-surpassed-1-4-million-players-during-its-advanced-access-period/)). Horizon 5 then sold an estimated **5.1 million copies for over $300 million on PS5** three and a half years after its Xbox launch, and a 50% sale lifted daily PS5 sales 3.4 times ([Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/forza-horizon-5-races-past-5m-copies)). Discounts are a real revenue tool late in a game's life, not a sign of failure. Paid early access earns well at this scale but draws criticism as an "early access tax" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/forza-horizon-6-game-pass-early-access-price-increase-premium-edition-60-120-2000662606)).

GT7 shows the value of sequencing trust before money. Its first paid DLC, the Power Pack, arrived only after almost four years of free updates, targeted committed players with 50 structured events, and bundled 5 million credits ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/gran-turismo-7-power-pack-details-20251203/)). Partners add both income and content: Toyota, Mazda, Fanatec, MOZA, Thrustmaster and Dunlop all hold official roles ([gran-turismo.com](https://www.gran-turismo.com/gb/news/00_8470834.html)), though fee values are not public.

Real car licences are a cost and a clock. Horizon 4 was delisted in December 2024 "due to licensing and agreements with our partners" ([Forza Support](https://support.forza.net/hc/en-us/articles/30662484986387-Forza-Horizon-4-Delisting-FAQ)), and EA has delisted five older Need for Speed titles because maintaining servers was "no longer feasible" ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/ea-pulls-five-need-for-speed-games-from-storefronts-as-maintaining-servers-is-no-longer-feasible/)).

Brand alone does not sustain revenue. Need for Speed has no game in development as of November 2026, with Criterion working solely on Battlefield ([Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/07/15/need-for-speed-faces-an-uncertain-future-after-criterions-battlefield-move/)). Its mobile game, No Limits, has an estimated 320 million-plus installs but only about "$100K+" in estimated monthly revenue ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.ea.game.nfs14_row)): a famous name drives downloads far more than spending.

## Every major backlash came from blocking the player's car

The failures are consistent enough to treat as rules. Forza Motorsport (2023) made players grind hours in each car to unlock its upgrade parts; Turn 10 halved the time within a week and removed the gate entirely five months later ([Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/forza-motorsports-latest-update-changes-how-car-progression-works)). Its Steam launch peak was 4,703 concurrent players against 81,096 for Horizon 5 ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/10/11/forza-motorsport-launch-peak-ccu-steam-mixed-reviews)), and Turn 10 ended new development in December 2025 ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2025/12/17/turn-off-the-engine-and-retire-the-car-turn-10-signals-end-of-forza-motorsport-support/)). Progression was not the only cause: PC performance, penalties and AI also featured in complaints.

Need for Speed Payback (2017) delivered upgrades as random Speed Cards that did not transfer between cars, and its studio raised payouts within weeks ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2017/11/need_for_speed_payback_alters_progression_amid_criticism)). The 2015 reboot's always-online, no-pause design drew petitions ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/speeds-online-requirement-starts-petitions-complaints/amp/)). Unbound kept solo and online garages separate, which reviewers named a major retention problem ([RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/need-for-speed/is-need-for-speed-unbound-dead/)).

GT7's March 2022 crisis is the sharpest case. A patch cut race payouts while credit packs were on sale, a roughly 30-hour server outage made the mostly online-dependent game unplayable, and it was review-bombed to what was reported as the lowest Metacritic user score of any PlayStation-published game ([KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/matthew-wilson/gran-turismo-7-now-has-the-lowest-user-score-of-any-playstation-game-ever/)). Polyphony apologised, gave every owner 1,000,000 credits and raised rewards ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/44911/gran-turismo-7-is-sorry-will-give-1m-free-credits-and-make-serious-changes)), then spent four years loosening the economy, ending with unlimited purchase invitations in 2025 and a Car Concierge in 2026 ([gran-turismo.com](https://www.gran-turismo.com/hk/news/00_3795674.html)). The driving was praised throughout; only the economy generated lasting hostility.

Tradeable economies add a further burden. Horizon 5's auction house was closed in 2022 after a credit-farming exploit ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/forza-horizon-5-auction-house-closed-after-players-managed-to-trash-the-games-economy)), and a July 2026 Horizon 6 update again had to close exploits ([ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/gaming/feature/forza-horizon-6s-latest-update-just-crashed-its-economy/)).

## What a small independent drag racing game should take from this

### Drag racing already has a proven template, and it is a meta-game

CSR Racing 2 has earned an estimated **$859.3 million in ten years** and still at least $3 million a month ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/zyngas-csr-2-celebrates-10th-anniversary-with-530m-cars-collected/)). Zynga credits short, intense races that fit brief sessions and tuning that is accessible but deep ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/a-billion-dollar-ride-csr-racing-game-franchise-hits-a-big-milestone-exclusive/)). The original CSR Racing supplies a pacing rule worth copying directly: a player needed three to seven wins to afford the next part and a session held ten races, so most sessions ended with a new part fitted ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog//2012/07/csr-racing-dissected.html)). Size the session so one sitting reliably buys one meaningful improvement. The original could be finished in 30 days; CSR 2 was rebuilt as a "digital hobby" around collecting and constant updates ([Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/after-seven-years-and-over-500m-naturalmotions-csr-2-is-still-shifting-gears/)). The campaign is the opening act; the collection and event layer is the long game.

### Build the loop the big three converge on

Start the player in a weak car and make early upgrades large. Drag racing suits this better than circuit racing because the result is one number, elapsed time, that visibly drops with each part. Make upgrades deterministic and plannable with one main currency, and make them change how the car looks or sounds: Katkoff lists visible upgrades, estimable milestones and player-chosen upgrade paths as the fixes for a grindy racing meta ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog//2016/01/need-for-speed-no-limits-and-why-racing.html)). Let any part be bought for any owned car from the start, and gate progress with money, classes and opponents instead; this is the direct Forza Motorsport lesson, and it matters more in a genre where tuning is the game.

Give the player a ladder. Most Wanted's Blacklist, Horizon 6's wristbands and GT7's menus all map onto drag racing as named rivals and elapsed-time classes. Add medal tiers on short tests of reaction, launch and shift timing as the equivalent of GT's licence tests, and themed collection sets as the equivalent of Café menus. A wager that rides with a multiplier until the player banks it is the drag version of Heat's night loop, provided the starter car can survive it. Drag itself is a proven request: its return was among the wins EA listed for Unbound ([EA](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/games/need-for-speed/need-for-speed-unbound/news/end-of-kaizen-review)).

### Keep the economy honest from day one

Never cut payouts after launch; if income is too generous, add new things to buy. Prefer stated rewards to random wheels, and if randomness exists, show the odds and offer a guaranteed route. Keep single-player fully offline and keep one garage across every mode. Give every time-limited item a later route back. Treat player trading as optional and risky: sharing tunes and liveries gives the social benefit without an economy to police.

Avoid the free-to-play energy and gacha model. Energy earned under 20% of CSR 1's revenue by one analyst's estimate ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog//2012/07/csr-racing-dissected.html)), while CSR 2 players complain of pay-gated event cars and being "bombarded with deals/bundles" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id887947640)). That model also depends on scale a small studio lacks: pooled 2025 mobile medians are about 22% day-1, 4% day-7 and 0.7% day-30 retention ([GameAnalytics via Game Industry Library](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/gameanalytics-mobile-retention-benchmarks-2026)). This benchmark comes from a search summary, not the full report.

### Pick a business model that fits a small team

The evidence favours a premium price, planned seasonal discounts and a later optional pack of structured events or cars, following GT7's Power Pack. Independent successes point the same way: art of rally sold an estimated 170,500 Steam copies for about $2.6 million ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/550320/art_of_rally)), and BeamNG.drive, a physics sandbox with heavy modding, averaged 20,660 concurrent Steam players in July 2026 ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/an-obscure-physics-sim-is-reeling-in-almost-as-many-players-as-forza-horizon-6-on-steam)). Both figures are tracker estimates read from summaries. Use fictional or lookalike cars: this avoids licence fees, manufacturer content rules and forced delisting. If mobile free-to-play is still the goal, Nitro Nation is the model: six core live-ops staff, a public monthly event calendar, community-requested cars and a rule against making players' past investments obsolete ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/creative-mobile-nitro-nation-seven-years-on/)).

Set an update rhythm the team can hold indefinitely: a weekly set of three to five bracket or class challenges built from existing content, one or two new cars or parts on a fixed schedule, and one named larger update a year.

## Conclusion

The surprise in this evidence is how little of these franchises' longevity depends on what a small studio cannot afford. Open worlds, photorealism and licensed car lists drive launch scale, but the retention work is done by legible ladders, plannable upgrades, small regular goals and an economy the player trusts. Those cost design discipline, not budget. Drag racing suits them unusually well: the race is seconds long, the result is a single number, and every part bought moves it.

Two things remain unknown. First, nothing here measures these mechanics at indie scale; every data point comes from franchises with millions of players, and no premium drag racing game has documented sales success. Second, with Need for Speed dormant and no successor announced, tuner-culture arcade racing looks under-served, but that is an inference from the franchise's status, not a sourced market finding. The practical test is cheap: ship the core loop with a ladder and a weekly challenge set, and watch whether players come back for the second week.
